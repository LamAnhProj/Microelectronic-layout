01

Where do resistance and coupling appear?

Read each extracted SPICE line as element · first node · second node · value. A capacitor between two signal nodes is coupling. A capacitor from a node to ground is ground capacitance. A wire resistor sits between two points along a conducting path.

The real coupon's R0 GND GND.t0 0.562404 is on its ground return. C0 connects two M4 nodes, while C1 connects M4 to ground. Those entries show how to read PEX; they do not measure the full RCO signal path.

Try it: what happens if the neighboring wire switches the other way?

The voltage difference across the coupling capacitor changes faster. Its current is i = Ccouple × d(VRC − Vaggressor)/dt, so it can slow or disturb the RC-node edge. Correlate a waveform kink with the neighbor's transition and the extracted netlist before assigning a cause.

02

When does the RC node cross the inverter threshold?

The red line is a 0.868752 V DC trip reference from the same SKY130A inverter MOS geometry at nominal 25 °C. Find where RC0 crosses it, then interpolate between the two neighboring waveform samples.

t_cross = t_a + (Vtrip − V_a)/(V_b − V_a) × (t_b − t_a)RC0 rises through 0.868752 V at 68.867500 µs.

The 0.900 V half-supply reference is crossed at 68.889580 µs, about 22.08 ns later. The DC trip is a static reference; dynamic switching can move with slew, load, supply, and corner.

Try it: which waveform follows RC0's rising threshold crossing?

RC1 falls through the same reference at 69.208670 µs, 341.170 ns after RC0's rise. The buffered CLK rises at 69.212286 µs. Keep the edge directions in your notes.

03

How do frequency, phase, and trim steps change?

Extra R or C generally makes the RC ramp shallower and moves its threshold crossing later. The left plot predicts that direction with a simple RC model. The right plot shows real nominal GF180MCU D schematic trim results.

Frequencyf = 1/TSKY saved clock: T = 1.955778 µs, f = 511.305 kHz.
Phaseφ = 360° × Δt/TRC0↑ to RC2↑: Δt = 0.651987 µs, φ = 120.01°.
Trim stepΔf = f(code+1) − f(code)GF code 116→117: −3.101 kHz; 117→118: −1.297 kHz.

The unequal GF steps already exist in the schematic model; they are not evidence of wire parasitics. Post-layout comparison must use the same PDK, code, corner, and stimulus.

Try it: what would make a coupling explanation convincing?

Look for a repeatable RC-node kink or crossing-time change aligned with a neighboring wire edge. Locate a capacitor between those two nets in the extracted network, then compare before and after routing or shielding. A frequency shift alone does not identify which parasitic caused it.
